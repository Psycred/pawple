Founder clarification — critical product principle for mating:

Pawple mating must be fundamentally pet-centric and mating-centric, not merely a pet-matching feature.

Do not approach this as Tinder, Hinge, Bumble, or any conventional dating/matching product adapted for pets. Swipe mechanics, dating-card patterns, superficial compatibility presentation, or other familiar dating-app conventions should not become the default simply because they are technically easy to implement.

The experience should feel genuinely native to Pawple and unlike anything users have experienced elsewhere.

The pet — its identity, breed, sex, age, personality, life, history, Moments, temperament, location, and responsible breeding intent — should be the centre of the experience. The human is the facilitator and responsible pet parent, not the person dating.

The product should focus on the act and purpose of finding a suitable mating opportunity for the pet, rather than maximising the number of matches.

"Match" should therefore not become the product's conceptual centre. Discovery, suitability, trust, responsible intent, mutual interest, and eventually facilitating an appropriate introduction should define the experience.

The emotional experience should remain distinctly Pawple: calm, warm, intentional, premium, trustworthy, private and pet-first. It should not feel gamified, addictive, transactional, or like a dating marketplace.

The current Beta criteria — breed, sex and user-selected geographic distance — are deliberately constrained matching inputs. They should not dictate the eventual interaction model.

Before engineering begins, establish the product experience and mental model for Pawple mating. We should be able to explain why this experience could not simply exist as a feature inside Tinder/Hinge or as a generic pet-matching app.

The objective is not merely to show users eligible pets. The objective is to create a meaningful, responsible and distinctly Pawple way for pet parents to discover potential mating opportunities for their pets.

Treat this as a core product-design principle for PAW-2 and any subsequent mating UX/architecture work.
